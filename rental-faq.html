Rental & Roommate FAQs

Helpful starting points for finding a room, sharing a home and renting in the United States.

Can I rent a room without renting an entire apartment?

Yes. Some homeowners and tenants offer private rooms in shared homes. Confirm that the person is allowed to rent the room, that the arrangement follows the lease and local rules, and that you understand shared-space expectations.

What should I ask before paying a room deposit?

Ask to tour the actual property, verify the person’s authority, review the written agreement, understand the deposit terms and get a receipt. Avoid irreversible or unusual payment methods and do not let urgency replace verification.

How do I find a compatible roommate?

Compare budget, move-in date, daily schedules, cleanliness, guests, pets, smoking, noise, shared costs and communication style. Discuss deal-breakers early and put shared expectations in writing.

What is usually included in room rent?

It varies. Ask specifically about electricity, gas, water, trash, internet, laundry, parking, furniture, kitchen access and storage. Get included services and extra charges in writing.

Do I need a roommate agreement?

A written agreement is useful for clarifying shared costs, chores, guests, quiet hours and move-out expectations. It should not conflict with or replace the lease.

What documents may a landlord request?

Depending on local law and the landlord’s process, applicants may be asked for identity, income, rental history, references or screening consent. Ask what is required and use a secure channel for sensitive documents.

How can I avoid rental scams?

Verify the property and the person, see the actual home, compare the price with similar rentals, read the agreement and avoid pressure to pay before verification. Never use gift cards, crypto or wire transfers to an unverified contact.

Are security deposit and application fee rules the same in every state?

No. Rules vary by state and sometimes city. Check official state or local housing resources before paying fees or signing an agreement.

Can a landlord refuse an applicant for any reason?

Housing providers must follow federal, state and local fair housing laws. Protected classes and additional local protections may apply. For a specific situation, consult an official housing agency or qualified legal professional.

What should I check during a rental tour?

Check locks, windows, water, heating and cooling, appliances, smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms where required, signs of leaks or pests, phone signal, common areas and the route to transit or parking.

How do I compare two rental listings?

Compare total monthly cost, move-in expenses, location, commute, lease length, room or property condition, included utilities, rules, availability and the credibility of the person offering it.

Does RentNest guarantee every listing?

No online marketplace can guarantee every listing or interaction. Use the platform’s reporting tools, independently verify the property and person, tour before paying and read all agreements carefully.

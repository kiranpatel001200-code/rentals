Rental safety tips

How to avoid rental scams, whether you rent or list a home.

Last updated: October 10, 2026

Contents
  1. For renters
  2. For landlords
  3. Report anything suspicious
  4. What we can and cannot do

For renters

  • See it in person (or have someone you trust see it) before you pay anything. A real landlord expects that.
  • Never send money to someone you have not met. Do not pay by wire transfer, gift cards, crypto, or cash apps to a stranger. These cannot be undone.
  • Read the lease before you sign, and check that the person who signs it owns or manages the home. You can look up ownership in county property records.
  • Be careful if the rent is far below similar homes, or if the landlord says they are out of the country, wants you to move fast, or refuses to meet.
  • Do not give your Social Security number or bank login before you have seen the home and a lease. Only share what an application really needs.
  • Keep your messages on RentNest at first, so there is a record.

For landlords

  • Meet applicants in person, or by video, and check their ID before you hand over keys.
  • Use a written lease, and keep receipts for any deposit and rent.
  • Follow fair housing rules. Treat every applicant the same way.
  • Do not share more of your personal details than you need to.

Report anything suspicious

Open the listing and use Report this listing. We review reports, and a listing with several reports is hidden while we look into it. If you lost money, contact your bank and report it to the Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

What we can and cannot do

We verify email addresses and limit abuse, but we cannot check every listing or person. Please use these tips every time. See also our Terms of Service.

What’s Your 20? Meaning, Origin & How Americans Use It

RentNest Editorial Team · Updated November 6, 2026 · 5-minute read

If someone asks, “What’s your 20?”, they usually want to know one simple thing: Where are you right now? It is a casual American expression with roots in radio communication. Here’s what it means, how people use it and how you can answer naturally.

“What’s your 20?”

Meaning: “What’s your location?” or simply “Where are you?”

What does “What’s your 20?” mean?

“What’s your 20?” is an informal way to ask someone’s current location. The “20” refers to the radio code 10-20, which means a person’s location or position. You might hear the phrase in a movie, in a conversation influenced by trucker or police-radio culture, or as a playful way to ask a friend where they are.

It is not a phrase everyone uses every day. Many Americans will simply say “Where are you?” or “Where are you at?” The expression can sound familiar, playful or old-school depending on the speaker and situation.

Where did the phrase come from?

The expression comes from the family of radio shorthand codes known as “10-codes.” These codes were developed to make two-way radio messages shorter and easier to communicate. In that system, 10-20 referred to location. The phrase became associated with Citizens Band (CB) radio users and truck-driver conversations, where quick location updates were useful.

Over time, “What’s your 20?” escaped radio chatter and became a recognizable expression in American pop culture. Today, people may use it jokingly or casually even when they are not using a radio.

How do Americans use it in a conversation?

Example 1 · Friends meeting up

Alex: “We’re at the coffee shop. What’s your 20?”
Jordan: “I’m two blocks away.”

Example 2 · A quick check-in

Sam: “Hey, what’s your 20?”
Riley: “Just got home.”

Example 3 · Radio-style joking

Casey: “We’re ready to head out. What’s your 20?”
Taylor: “Parked outside. 10-4!”

How can you answer “What’s your 20?”

You can answer with a general place or a short update. You do not need to share a precise address.

  • “I’m at the office.”
  • “Just leaving home.”
  • “I’m near downtown.”
  • “About five minutes away.”
  • “I’m at the front entrance.”
Privacy tip: Share your exact location only with someone you trust and when it is appropriate. If the person is unfamiliar, a general area is usually enough.

Is “What’s your 20?” formal or informal?

It is informal. It can work among friends, in playful texts, or in conversations where both people understand the expression. In a work email, a formal appointment, or a message to someone you do not know, use a clearer question such as “Where are you currently?” or “Could you share your location?”

Is it connected to “10-4”?

Yes. Both expressions are associated with radio shorthand. “10-4” is commonly understood as “message received” or “understood,” while “10-20” refers to location. You may hear them together in radio-themed dialogue, but they do not mean the same thing.

Can you use “What’s your 20?” when looking for a room or roommate?

You can use it casually after you have started talking with someone—for example, when coordinating a room tour or meeting a potential roommate at a public place. But it is not a standard rental-search term. When contacting a landlord or responding to a room listing, clearer questions are better: “What neighborhood is the room in?” or “Where should we meet for the viewing?”

If you are arranging a viewing, confirm the meeting place in advance, tell someone you trust where you are going and avoid sharing sensitive personal details with strangers.
